# What is the Problem?

Imagine visiting a library for the first time. Most people can look at a floor plan and see where the rooms, stairs, and exits are. A blind visitor may not have that option.

A tactile map is a raised floor plan that someone can read with their fingertips. It can help them understand a building before moving through it. But making one often means hiring a specialist to redraw the plan, add braille, and prepare it for printing. That takes time and money, so many smaller organizations go without one.

The result is that people who need a clear map of an unfamiliar building often cannot get one. We want tactile maps to become a normal part of making buildings accessible.
